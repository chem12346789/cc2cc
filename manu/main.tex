\section{Introduction}
The well-known Kohn--Sham equation \cite{kohnSelfConsistentEquationsIncluding1965-11-15} is
\begin{align}
	\label{eq:KS}
	\Big( - \frac{1}{2} \nabla^{2} + v_{\mathrm{ext}} + v_{\mathrm{H}} + v_{\mathrm{XC}}\Big) \phi_{i} = \epsilon_{i} \phi_{i}.
\end{align}
% 
Where $\phi_{i}$ is the Kohn--Sham orbital, $\epsilon_{i}$ is the Kohn--Sham orbital energy, $v_{\mathrm{ext}}$ is the external potential (such as the electrostatic potential of nuclei), $v_{\mathrm{H}}$ is the electrostatic potential of the electron, and $v_{\mathrm{XC}}$ is the exchange-correlation potential.
% 
According to the Hohenberg-Kohn theorem \cite{hohenbergInhomogeneousElectronGas1964-11-09}, exchange-correlation energy density $e_{\mathrm{XC}}$ is the unique functional of the ground-state electron density, $\rho = n_{i} \vert \phi_{i} \vert^{2}$, where $n_{i}$ is the occupation number of the Kohn-Sham orbital.
%
Then, the Kohn-Sham equation is solved iteratively utilizing the so-called potential term $v_{\mathrm{XC}} = \delta E_{\mathrm{XC}} / \delta \rho$.
% 
So, once this unique functional is obtained, the properties of the molecules can be calculated accurately and efficiently.
%
This is the main idea of the density functional theory (DFT) \cite{parrDensityFunctionalTheory1980}.

One of the challenging tasks of the DFT calculation is to find the unique functional between the exact exchange-correlation energy density $e_{\mathrm{XC}}$ and the electron density $\rho$.
% 
Numerous approximations have been proposed for this functional, including early local-density approximation functionals \cite{parrDensityFunctionalTheory1980}, hybrid functionals \cite{perdewAtomsMoleculesSolids1992-09-15}, and the rapidly growing class of machine-learning (ML) functionals \cite{nagaiCompletingDensityFunctional2020-05-05,chenDeePKSComprehensiveDataDriven2021-01-12,kirkpatrickPushingFrontiersDensity2021-12-10,luiseAccurateScalableExchangecorrelation2025-06-23,anMitigatingErrorCancellation2025-08-07}.

In this article, we combine the machine learning method and density functional theory (DFT) to predict the properties of the molecules such as the energy, the dipole, the bond length, the bond dissociation energy, the band gap, and so on.


\section{The NEURAL NETWORK}
We use the transformer \cite{vaswaniAttentionAllYou2023-08-02} as the neural network to learn the functional between the electron density and the energy density of the molecule.
% 
After the training, the neural network can predict the energy of the molecule given the electron density and then can be used to optimize the electron density iteratively using the self-consistent field (SCF) method.

The architecture of the neural network is shown in \Fig{fig:arch}.
%
The input of the neural network is a set of the energy density ${\tilde{\bm{e}}_{\rm{cube}}(\brm{r}_i)}$ with the size of $(N, 27, 4)$, and the output is the energy density ${e(\brm{r}_i)}$ with the size of $(N, 1)$.
% 
N is the number of the grid points in the normal dft procedure.
%
\begin{align}
	\mathbf{r}_{i} =
	 & (x_{i},y_{i},z_{i})                                                                                 \\
	\tilde{\bm{e}}_{\rm{cube}}(\brm{r}_{i}) =
	 & \big\{ \tilde{e}(\mathbf{r}'_{i}) \text{ for } \mathbf{r}'_{i} \in \mathbf{r}_{i;\rm{cube}} \big\}. \\
	\tilde{e}(\brm{r}) =
	 & (e_{\rm{LDA}}(\brm{r}), e_{\rm{VWN3}}(\brm{r}), e_{\rm{B88}}(\brm{r}), e_{\rm{LYP}}(\brm{r})),      \\
	\mathbf{r}_{i;\rm{cube}} =
	 & \big\{ \mathbf{r}'_{i} = (x',y',z') \vert \nl
	 & x' \in [x_{i}-d,x_{i},x_{i}+d], \nl
	 & y' \in [y_{i}-d,y_{i},y_{i}+d], \nl
	 & z' \in [z_{i}-d,z_{i},z_{i}+d] \big\}.
\end{align}
The $\tilde{e}(\brm{r})$ is the energy density calculated by the 4 functionals (LDA, VWN3, B88 and LYP) from the original B3LYP functional \cite{perdewAtomsMoleculesSolids1992-09-15}.
% 
The $\mathbf{r}_{i;\rm{cube}}$ is a cube around the grid point $\mathbf{r}_{i}$ with the size of $3 \times 3 \times 3$ and the distance between two adjacent grid points is $d$.
%
This means that for each grid point $\mathbf{r}_{i}$, we consider a small region around it to capture the local environment and compute the energy density more accurately.
% 
We refer to the grid points within the cube, excluding the central point, as auxiliary grid points. Details of their contributions to the energy density are provided in the Appendix.
% 
The output of the neural network is the energy density at the grid point $\mathbf{r}_{i}$, denoted as $e(\brm{r}_{i})$.
% 
The total exchange-correlation energy is obtained by integrating the energy density over the entire grid.

\subsection{DATASET AND THE LOSS FUNCTION}
The dataset we use to train the neural network is listed in the Appendix.
%
We use 551 molecules which consistent of less than 3 heavy atoms as the training set and 115 molecules contain 4-5 heavy atoms as the validation set.
% 
In this article, we use the CCSD(T)/Def2-QZVP (except the anions) and CCSD(T)/Ma-Def2-QZVP (the anions) \cite{bartlettCoupledclusterTheoryQuantum2007-02-22,weigendBalancedBasisSets2005,zhengMinimallyAugmentedKarlsruhe2011-02} to calculate the electron density and the energy density of the molecules.
%
The electron density will be used as the calculation of the input of the neural network and the energy density will be used as the target of the neural network.

The loss function is defined as
\begin{align}
	\label{eq:loss}
	L_{e} =
	 & \lambda_{\rm{RE}} N_{\rm{atom}} \Big(\int \d \brm{r}_{i} \big(\bar{e}(\brm{r}_{i}) - e(\brm{r}_{i})\big) \Big)^2 \nl
	 & + \lambda_{\rm{ABS}} N_{\rm{atom}} \Big(\int \d \brm{r}_{i} \Big| \bar{e}(\brm{r}_{i}) - e(\brm{r}_{i}) \Big| \Big)^2 \nl
	 & + \lambda_{\rm{AE}} N_{\rm{atom}} \Big(\bar{E}^{\rm{AE}}(\brm{r}_{i}) - E^{\rm{AE}}(\brm{r}_{i}) \Big)^2 .
\end{align}
where $N_{\rm{atom}}$ is the number of atoms in the molecule and $E^{\rm{AE}}$ and $\bar{E}^{\rm{AE}}$ is the atomic energy of the predicted and target values, respectively.
%
The $\lambda_{\rm{RE}}$, $\lambda_{\rm{ABS}}$, and $\lambda_{\rm{AE}}$ are the weights of the three terms in the loss function.
%
The first term is the relative error in the total energy, the second term is the absolute error in the total energy, and the third term is the error in the atomic energy.
